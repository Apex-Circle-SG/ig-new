# People, leadership and management upload: editorial review

Reviewed 6 November 2026. Review and recommendations only; no catalogue, page or deployment changes.

## Finding

This is a mixed collection, with a strong core in leadership, organizational change and people management. It also contains executive performance measures, critical thinking, productivity, spiritual beliefs and humorous personality narratives. Publishing it all under HR or Project Management would obscure its meaning.

Expand the existing **Leadership & Organizational Change** hub into **Leadership, People & Change**, with focused collections beneath it. Place the other subjects in their existing specialist destinations and cross-link them. Keep Business Management as a compact entrance rather than returning to a long catch-all list.

## Important editorial decisions and gaps

- **KPIs are names, mostly not formulas.** There are 314 role/metric occurrences across CEO, CFO, COO, revenue leadership, an HR list and CMO. Repeated metrics should have one canonical explanation and retain their role associations. Do not invent formulas, benchmark values or scoring systems for readiness, ESG, happiness and similar indices.
- **Two different CRO headings:** the first clearly concerns revenue; the second contains HR metrics. “CHRO / people leadership” is a plausible editorial correction to the second, but must be marked as inferred while preserving the source heading. It should not silently become Chief Risk Officer material.
- **Mixed and mistyped metric labels:** source financial/nonfinancial bins contain mismatches, and several abbreviations or words are corrupted. Correct only where meaning is secure; retain original wording in provenance and flag unresolved terms.
- **Two missing downloads:** Human Resource paragraphs 183–184 are unavailable-attachment placeholders. The text does contain 26 SBTI profiles, but no complete questionnaire or scoring algorithm. Do not invent a quiz from the narratives.
- **An unfinished excerpt:** Human Resource paragraph 8 ends “…research, and visits to peer”. Retain and flag the incomplete passage; do not complete it by guesswork.
- **Feedback matrix:** the supplied awareness matrix resembles other feedback models but uses its own labels and additional stages. Explain the supplied version; do not silently claim it is the canonical Johari Window.
- **Energy means different things here:** the 20-item workplace reflection checklist and its supplied score bands are separate from Hawkins's spiritual hierarchy. Do not combine their scores. Source labels such as “burned out” must not become a clinical diagnosis.
- **Leadership models need context:** retain comparisons and authored principles without making managers caricatures or treating leadership “laws” as scientific laws. Situational-leadership variants and their axes need attribution before reconstruction.
- **Satirical and demeaning animal labels:** preserve the source metaphors in a clearly contextualized section, but use neutral performance/commitment language in professional guidance. Do not turn a quadrant into an automatic firing recommendation.
- **Dated statistics and quotations:** the UK workplace/AI forecasts, lifestyle percentages, income quadrants and several quotations lack adequate dates or citations. Retain as attributed source notes with uncertainty, not current statistics, pay promises or verified quotations.
- **Duplication:** Change Management's diagnosis/pace/scope discussion repeats substantially at paragraphs 80–84 and 98–101. Consolidate the explanation while retaining both source references.

4. Keep original metric labels, profile descriptions, diagram meanings and scoring bands traceable. Explicitly list missing attachments, incomplete text and uncertain attribution.
5. Update catalogue relationships, canonical links, the combined sitemap and the human-readable content index in the same implementation. Preserve existing URLs where practical.
6. Check coverage, category links, mobile/desktop readability and diagram sizes. Use existing consent-aware advertising components. Content review does not require new automated tests; functional directory/filter changes warrant appropriate behavior checks.
7. Commit only the intended implementation and reports. Push only when separately requested.
