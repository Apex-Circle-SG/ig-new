# Deal terminology: legal, accounting and listing source review

Checked 6 November 2026. Bounded editorial research for the supplied deal material; no contract drafting or assessment of a reader's transaction. Jurisdictions and frameworks below are deliberate limits. Existing calculator methodology and historical-case reports remain the source for other finance and transaction claims.

## Preliminary agreements: the title does not decide enforceability

**Suggested proposition:** “A term sheet or letter of intent records proposed terms. Its binding effect depends on the language, agreed obligations, surrounding documents, facts and governing law; some provisions or preliminary duties may bind even where the final transaction remains uncommitted.” Avoid both “all LOIs are binding” and “term sheets are never legally binding.”

**Primary authority / scope:** Delaware Superior Court, *GreenTech Consultancy Co. v Hilco IP Services*, 11 May 2022, pp. 32–35, applies Delaware preliminary-agreement doctrine. It distinguishes a fully binding preliminary agreement from an agreement requiring good-faith negotiation of open terms without committing the parties to the ultimate transaction. This is a Delaware example, not a universal international rule. [Court opinion](https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=332770).

## Share purchases, asset purchases and dissolution

**Suggested proposition:** “A share acquisition changes ownership of the target's shares. An asset acquisition transfers the specified assets and agreed obligations. Neither label alone says that the selling or acquired legal entity automatically dissolves. A merger or subsequent winding-up is a distinct step.” Do not define every acquisition as requiring more than 50% ownership: minority-stake purchases also exist; distinguish acquisition of an interest from acquisition of control.

**Primary authority / scope:** Delaware General Corporation Law §271 provides procedures for a sale of all or substantially all assets; §275 separately provides dissolution procedures. Their separation supports the bounded point that an asset sale is not itself automatic dissolution. Do not generalise the precise approvals, exceptions or liability allocation to other jurisdictions. [Delaware statute, §§271 and 275](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc10/index.html).

**First-party example:** JLR describes itself as a wholly owned subsidiary of Tata Motors since 2008, illustrating that a full share acquisition can leave the target operating as a subsidiary. The verified historical transaction evidence is already in `deal-case-verification.json`. [JLR corporate overview](https://www.jlr.com/overview).

## Pro rata rights and anti-dilution protection

**Suggested proposition:** “Pro rata participation rights allow an investor, subject to their terms, to invest additional money in a later financing to maintain a proportionate holding. Price-based anti-dilution terms can adjust a security's conversion price or ratio following specified issuances. These are different rights; neither guarantees that an investor can never be diluted.”

**Primary model author / scope:** YC's current US post-money SAFE materials describe an optional pro rata side letter, rather than an automatic right in every SAFE. Coverage and exercise conditions follow the particular instrument. [YC SAFE materials, pro rata section](https://www.ycombinator.com/safe).

**Primary issuer example / scope:** Redwire's 2023 preliminary information statement, Annex A §7.6(i), describes a conversion-price adjustment for defined lower-price issuances, with exclusions and its own trigger. It illustrates a contractual mechanism, not a standard mandatory formula or SEC endorsement. [Redwire filing on SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819810/000114036123028964/ny20009339x1_pre14c.htm). NVCA's October 2025 model charter is also a model starting point, not a statute; its direct DOCX was publicly linked but could not be retrieved in this review, so no uninspected clause is relied upon. [NVCA models](https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/).

## Vesting and exercise

**Suggested proposition:** “Vesting concerns satisfaction of conditions attached to an award or retention of its benefits. Exercising an option means using its contractual right to acquire shares, generally at an exercise price. Vesting does not itself mean an option has been exercised, that shares were purchased, or that a profit has been realised. Some plans permit early exercise before vesting, subject to restrictions.”

**Primary company / scope:** Navan's FY2026 annual report, share-based compensation note, describes early exercise of certain unvested options and a company repurchase right that lapses as shares vest. This demonstrates why the terms cannot be treated as synonyms; its particular four-year schedule and other conditions are company-specific. [Navan FY2026 report on EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1639723/000162828026034098/navan-fy2026ars.pdf). US tax consequences vary by instrument and are a separate topic; the IRS itself distinguishes grant and exercise events. [IRS Topic 427](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc427).

## SAFE context

**Suggested proposition:** “A SAFE is a contractual instrument for future equity under specified events and terms. YC's post-money SAFE is a particular model family; it is not identical to a convertible promissory note or already-issued shares.” YC's comparison describes its SAFE as having no interest or maturity date, unlike a convertible note. Do not extend that description to every modified agreement bearing the SAFE name or promise repayment, guaranteed conversion, a fixed return, or universal tax/accounting treatment.

**Primary model author / date / scope:** YC's materials checked November 2026 distinguish cap-only, discount-only and uncapped MFN US forms and provide separate forms for certain other jurisdictions. Pre-money and post-money mechanics differ; the post-money cap is not a guarantee of an unchanged percentage after later priced financing or option-pool changes. [YC model downloads and explanations](https://www.ycombinator.com/safe); [YC comparison with notes and priced rounds](https://www.ycombinator.com/safe/safe-vs-convertible-note). These are model-author descriptions, not findings that a supplied contract matches the model.

## Nasdaq: specify market, pathway and rule date

**Suggested proposition:** “Nasdaq initial listing depends on the relevant market tier, security type, listing pathway, applicable financial/liquidity standards, corporate governance and other qualitative requirements, and Nasdaq's review. Satisfying a single numerical threshold does not establish eligibility or guarantee admission.”

**Primary exchange / date / jurisdiction:** Nasdaq US's **January 2026 Initial Listing Guide** distinguishes Global Select, Global Market and Capital Market standards. Use it as a dated overview and link current rules; IPOs, direct listings, acquisition companies and other pathways can have different conditions. Do not reproduce an isolated price, profit, shareholder-count or operating-history number as a universal IPO test. [Official guide](https://listingcenter.nasdaq.com/assets/initialguide.pdf).

The Rule 5600 series covers governance and qualitative matters including board/committee requirements, conduct, shareholder meetings, related-party review and shareholder approval; Rule 5615 includes exemptions and phase-ins. Rule 5101 reserves discretionary authority to deny or apply stricter criteria even if enumerated criteria are met. Link the relevant current rules instead of scoring “IPO ready” from a worksheet. [Current Rule 5600 series](https://listingcenter.nasdaq.com/rulebook/nasdaq/rules/nasdaq-5600-series); [Rule 5101 and interpretive material](https://listingcenter.nasdaq.com/rulebook/nasdaq/rules/Nasdaq%205100%20Series).

## Goodwill, identifiable assets and subsequent impairment

**Suggested proposition under full IFRS:** “IFRS 3 governs acquisition-date recognition and measurement of a business combination, including identifiable assets/liabilities and residual goodwill. IAS 36 governs subsequent impairment. Goodwill under full IFRS is not routinely amortised: it is tested at least annually and when impairment indicators arise. Separately identified finite-life intangible assets have different subsequent accounting under IAS 38.” A simplified purchase-price-minus-book-equity amount is not a complete purchase-price allocation; goodwill is not automatically the whole purchase premium.

**Primary standard setter / scope:** The existing methods report links the [IFRS 3 overview](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-3-business-combinations/). The [IAS 36 overview](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-36-impairment-of-assets/) confirms annual goodwill testing and that goodwill impairment is not reversed. The [IAS 38 overview](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-38-intangible-assets/) distinguishes finite-life amortisation and indefinite-life impairment.

The IFRS Foundation's **2025 educational Module 19**, comparison with full IFRS, explicitly contrasts IFRS 3/IAS 36's non-amortisation/annual impairment treatment with IFRS for SMEs' amortisation model. This supports using a named reporting framework rather than saying goodwill is always amortised or never amortised everywhere. [IFRS Foundation Module 19, comparison section p. 72](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/supporting-implementation/smes/2025-modules/module-19.pdf). Full standard PDFs may require IFRS registration/subscription; public overviews and the educational comparison are sufficient for this bounded correction. These are financial-reporting statements, not claims about tax deductions.
