## Recommendation: an academic area with two connected purposes

Create **Research Methods & Academic Skills** as a distinct library topic, with two entrances:

1. **Plan, conduct and report research** — a guided learning path from question to design, evidence, analysis, writing and limitations.
2. **Find academic sources** — a curated, searchable bibliography that connects the supplied readings to the subject guides already on the site.

This is broader than AI statistics and more specific than a business-planning checklist. It should not be placed wholesale in Finance, HR, or Project Management. The existing `/research/` area serves the site’s own research/publications; keep that purpose and link to the new learning area rather than silently replacing it with coursework notes.

### Proposed methods reading areas

| Reader intention | Content to integrate |
| --- | --- |
| Develop a defensible question | Research versus summary/consultancy, novelty, basic/applied inquiry, proposal components, feasibility and milestones |
| Choose an approach | Ontology/epistemology, induction/deduction, quantitative, qualitative and mixed methods; distinguish philosophical claims from universal procedural rules |
| Review existing work | Search strategy, literature mapping, definitions, arguments, method critique, synthesis and a literature/evidence matrix |
| Design data collection | Operationalization, reliability/validity, documents/secondary data, observation, interviews, probing and questionnaire design |
| Select and assess a sample | Population/frame/sample, probability versus non-probability designs, recruitment, non-response, sample-size justification and error sources |
| Analyse quantitative evidence | Measurement scales, descriptions, association/difference questions, model assumptions, paired/independent designs and conditional test comparisons |
| Interpret and report | Hypotheses, uncertainty, effect estimates, limitations, inference versus causality, and transparent analysis reporting |
| Use SPSS appropriately | A version-labelled workflow/checklist linked to the official documentation; this source is a study handout, not a complete SPSS manual or worked dataset |
| Write and reference | Paraphrase versus quotation, page/locator details, reference list versus bibliography and a consistent author–date style |
| Connect inquiry with practice | Case-study/action-research distinctions, involvement/reflexivity, consultancy recommendations, implementation ownership and impact |

Aim for coherent guides with sufficient detail, not a separate thin page for every test or citation. The final guide count should follow the overlap review.

## How the bibliography should work

Each reference should retain its supplied wording and workbook sheet/cell, alongside separately verified fields: author(s), year, title, publication type, journal/book/publisher, volume/issue/pages, DOI or stable source URL, edition/version and relevant subjects.

Use distinct states such as **metadata checked**, **incomplete**, **possible duplicate**, **abstract reviewed**, **full text reviewed**, and **supports this specific claim**. Finding a DOI is not evidence that the paper supports the claim beside it. A title can guide classification, but cannot justify an invented summary of results.

Useful reader features are subject/author/year/type filters, meaningful topic tags, copyable references and stable publisher/DOI links. Citation exports can be added when metadata is complete. Do not create hundreds of isolated reference pages or label the entire list peer-reviewed: it includes books, articles, professional reports, working papers, theses, web sources and journal recommendations.

Show a manageable starting selection and retain access to the complete catalogue. Organize the large HR list by meaning, for example strategic HRM and competitive advantage; business partnering and operating models; HR/line relationships; performance and measurement; power/legitimacy/influence; international/cross-cultural HR; and talent/skills. Research-method references inside that sheet belong in methods too, not exclusively HR.

## Methods claims needing correction or qualification

These notes are useful raw study material, but are not ready to publish as authoritative academic guidance without editorial correction:

- Quantitative research is not automatically objective, random, anonymous, generalizable or confined to artificial settings. Qualitative inquiry is not automatically incapable of generalization or restricted to words. Clarify analytic/transferability claims versus statistical population inference, and distinguish data form from research design.
- The blanket “80 per subgroup” and “maximum” qualitative sample-size statements lack the required context. Sample justification depends on the question, design, effect/precision, variation, analysis and feasibility; there is no supplied universal threshold.
- Several quantitative/qualitative pros/cons contradict each other or are misfiled. Some citations are blog-style or too incomplete to identify, such as a journal name/year without an article or a first-name-only attribution. Preserve them, but do not promote them to scholarly evidence without identification and checking.
- Reliability is not the same as validity or causal identification. Software does not make an analysis valid, fast or reproducible by itself; a cross-sectional association does not establish an intervention’s causal effect.
- The SPSS chooser incorrectly puts ordinary ANCOVA alongside Mann–Whitney in a nonparametric branch. Choice needs the outcome, design, covariates and assumptions. [IBM GLM documentation](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/spss-statistics/30.0.0?topic=statistics-glm-multivariate-analysis).
- The paired sign test should not be confused with independent-sample comparisons. Pearson correlation and regression are not distinguished by whether they “describe a relationship.” The source’s simple group-count rule is insufficient for distinguishing one-way versus factorial designs. [IBM related-samples documentation](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/spss-statistics/30.0.0?topic=nptests-related-subcommand-command).
- Statistical significance is not proof that a hypothesis is true or false, and a p-value is not the probability that an effect exists. Report estimates, uncertainty, assumptions and practical context. [American Statistical Association statement](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/P-valuestatement.pdf).
- Correct distinctions between nominal variables and category counts; interval versus ratio examples; a sampling fraction versus the systematic selection interval; and purposive versus convenience sampling.
- A domain suffix does not establish source reliability. Citation punctuation, quote conventions and bibliography/reference-list requirements depend on the stated style and institution; Google Scholar is a discovery service, not itself the primary source of every indexed claim.
- Course statements that every dissertation needs data collected from businesses, or that novelty means one particular theoretical contribution, should not be presented as universal academic requirements.
- Research ethics, informed participation, confidentiality and reproducible analysis records need explicit coverage as **editorial additions** where not developed in the upload. Link to the existing Ethics & Governance collection while distinguishing research-participant responsibilities from generic corporate ethics.
