## Cross-cultural frameworks: keep their constructs separate

**Supplied:** Teams ¶334–358 names Hofstede's six culture dimensions, GLOBE's nine
culture dimensions and six leadership dimensions. The list headings are distinct;
several abbreviated explanations need improvement.

**Suggested public paragraph:** Hofstede's national-culture model compares power
distance, individualism/collectivism, masculinity/femininity, uncertainty avoidance,
long-term/short-term orientation and indulgence/restraint. These are aggregate
comparisons with substantial variation within societies. The historical
masculinity/femininity labels concern societal expectations, not the traits or
abilities of an individual man or woman. Use them to raise contextual questions,
then ask people about their own preferences. See the
[Hofstede estate's model explanation](https://geerthofstede.com/culture-geert-hofstede-gert-jan-hofstede/6d-model-of-national-culture/).

Correct the upload's ¶339 reversal: long-term orientation is associated with
pragmatic adaptation; short-term orientation with normative/traditional reference.
Do not reduce uncertainty avoidance to risk appetite.

**Suggested public paragraph:** GLOBE distinguishes reported practices (“as is”)
from desired values (“should be”) across nine societal dimensions: performance,
assertiveness, future orientation, humane orientation, institutional collectivism,
in-group collectivism, gender egalitarianism, power distance and uncertainty
avoidance. Its separate leadership dimensions are charismatic/value-based,
team-oriented, participative, humane-oriented, autonomous and self-protective.
Do not exchange GLOBE and Hofstede scores or use national averages to predict an
employee's behaviour. See the
[GLOBE researchers' study overview](https://www.globeproject.com/study_2004_2007?page_id=data)
and [construct definitions](https://globeproject.com/data/GLOBE-Dimensions-Definitions-and-Scale-Items.pdf).

The individual-use caution is an editorial consequence of the aggregate unit of
comparison, not an individual diagnostic result from either framework.

## Team and leadership models

### Belbin

**Supplied:** Teams ¶376–385 includes all nine roles. **Checked:** the role set and
attribution agree with Belbin's official explanation.

**Suggested public paragraph:** Meredith Belbin's nine team roles describe
contributions through behaviour: Resource Investigator, Teamworker, Co-ordinator,
Plant, Monitor Evaluator, Specialist, Shaper, Implementer and Completer Finisher.
People can contribute in several roles; the task determines which behaviours are
needed. Nine roles do not require nine team members. Use the model as a vocabulary
for collaboration rather than fixed personality labels or a locally invented
test. [Belbin's official role guide](https://www.belbin.com/about/belbin-team-roles).

### Lencioni

**Supplied:** Teams ¶370–375 reproduces the five dysfunction names, with “of” in
place of “to” in the final name. **Checked:** the author-associated Table Group
confirms the model and explanations.

**Suggested public paragraph:** Patrick Lencioni's five dysfunctions are absence
of trust, fear of conflict, lack of commitment, avoidance of accountability and
inattention to results. Here, trust involves willingness to admit difficulties;
productive conflict concerns ideas; commitment means clarity and buy-in;
accountability concerns agreed behaviour; results means collective outcomes.
Use these as discussion themes, without inventing diagnostic cut-offs.
[The Table Group's original model overview](https://files.tablegroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/11224029/FiveDysfunctions.pdf).

### Bass and Spears: distinct models

**Supplied:** Teams ¶394–408 correctly separates four Bass components from ten
Spears characteristics, but “rust and admiration to work harder” is not a useful
definition of inspirational motivation.

**Suggested public paragraph:** Bass's transformational-leadership framework,
developed further with Bruce Avolio, describes idealised influence through example,
inspirational motivation through shared purpose, intellectual stimulation through
questioning assumptions, and individualised consideration through coaching and
development. The framework explanation is separate from the licensed Multifactor
Leadership Questionnaire; a short checklist here is not an MLQ result.
[Mind Garden, the instrument publisher, explains the components and lineage](https://www.mindgarden.com/documents/TransformationalLeadership.pdf).

The publisher's white paper directly describes the components and cites Bass;
its opening paragraph also quotes Wikipedia, which is **not** the basis for the
component clarification above. For primary bibliographic attribution, see
[Bass's 1990 paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0090-2616(90)90061-S); its full text was
not available in the browser preview and has not been used to claim detailed
wording verification.

**Suggested public paragraph:** Larry Spears distilled ten servant-leadership
characteristics from Robert Greenleaf's writings: listening, empathy, healing,
awareness, persuasion, conceptualisation, foresight, stewardship, commitment to
people's growth and building community. They are a reflective leadership
framework, not a complete list or a medical meaning of “healing”.
[Spears's own account](https://spearscenter.org/ten-characteristics-of-servant-leadership/)
and [his university-published article](https://www.regent.edu/journal/journal-of-virtues-leadership/character-and-servant-leadership-ten-characteristics-of-effective-caring-leaders/).

For the rest of the glossary, preserve these **editorial correction targets** and
complete focused source checking if publishing detailed definitions:

- Eigenstate/eigenvalue: define relative to a specified operator, not exclusively
  a differential or “wave” equation.
- Schrödinger equation: the evolution equation for a nonrelativistic quantum
  state; Hamiltonian: the energy operator in that formulation.
- Pauli matrices: three dimensionless matrices; spin-½ components are ħ/2 times
  those matrices. Do not equate an unscaled matrix with angular momentum.
- Feynman diagrams: perturbative interaction/calculation diagrams, extending well
  beyond one photon and one particle.
- Stern–Gerlach: avoid claiming the original experiment directly established the
  later electron-spin interpretation. Distinguish discrete angular-momentum
  measurement results from the historical interpretation.
- Spherical Bessel functions: the source is too abbreviated for a useful entry;
  avoid equating their equation with the ordinary Bessel equation without the
  appropriate transformation.

## Hormones, brain timing and grief

**Power poses — supplied:** Coaching ¶289 says a high-power pose increases
testosterone. **Checked:** Ranehill and colleagues' larger replication found no
effect on hormones or risk tolerance; this contradicts presenting the hormone
claim as established. [Original replication paper record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25810452/)
and [the authors' university research-centre summary](https://www.zne.uzh.ch/en/news/Ranehill-Power-Poses.html).

**Suggested public paragraph:** Expansive posture has been studied as a confidence
exercise, but the claim that a brief pose reliably raises testosterone is not
established. A larger replication did not reproduce the hormonal result. Do not
promise hormonal, medical or performance benefits from a posture exercise.

**Brain timing — supplied:** Coaching ¶284 asserts “0.3s neurotransmission + 0.2s
choice point” and a fixed regional hierarchy; ¶286 locates willpower in ACC and
recommends hard tasks to build it. **Status: unverified.** No study, population,
task or measurement method accompanies these statements. This pass did not locate
their originating experiment; do not retrofit unrelated neuroscience studies as
confirmation or preserve exact timing as a universal rule.

**Suggested public paragraph:** The source proposes pausing before responding and
practising deliberate choices. These are practical reflection suggestions; the
supplied half-second timing formula and single-region explanation have not been
verified and are not needed to use the exercise.

**Grief — supplied:** Coaching ¶11 substitutes “blame” for bargaining; ¶381 onward
uses bargaining and adds resurgence. **Checked:** the Kübler-Ross Foundation
explains that responses can overlap, repeat or be absent; the model is not a
mandatory sequence or timetable. [Foundation's explanation](https://www.ekrfoundation.org/five-stages-of-grief-tm/).

**Suggested public paragraph:** Denial, anger, bargaining, depression and acceptance
are familiar labels associated with Kübler-Ross's work. People do not have to
experience all five or experience them in order. Treat the labels as possible
descriptions, not a test, recovery deadline or prescribed path. “Resurgence” in
the supplied notes is an additional observation, not a verified sixth stage.

The supplied RAS explanation, cortisol/illness generalisations, painkiller claim,
brain-flow definition, resilience/depression spiral and other medical mechanisms
also remain unverified. Keep their exact text in provenance or identify them as
unverified source assertions; do not turn them into treatment guidance.
