# Sales, communication and strategy-classics collection

Status: implemented locally; not pushed.

## Coverage and organization

- New archive: six DOCX files, 1,658 nonempty document paragraphs, approximately 19,081 words and three images.
- Original uploaded bytes retained in `content/sources/business/sales/collection.zip`; each document and image is fingerprinted in its editorial inventory.
- 45 new topic/chapter guides, including all 13 supplied Art of War chapters.
- Three collection indexes: sales playbook, NLP reference and Art of War. One new top-level Communication & Personal Development hub.
- Four existing guides receive appended, semantically related material: growth methods, resource base, stakeholder management and the strategic-management cycle.
- The original management archive, inventory, coverage ledger and all 57 existing guides’ section/figure contents are preserved.
- The searchable sales question bank retains all 82 supplied question prompts, their section context and four conversation stages.
- Across both retained batches: 4,335 nonempty paragraphs and 23 images accounted for, with zero unmapped items. Existing source headings can be consolidated; identical historical verses may share a displayed paragraph while both source positions remain mapped.

The shared generation pipeline reads both fingerprinted archives together. Source-specific plans classify content; it does not infer categories from filenames or accept arbitrary replacement uploads. Every nonstructural paragraph is asserted present in its rendered detail model. The question bank has an additional check that every prompt and contextual label is present in its actual display model. Both source archives and all destinations are validated before generated files are written.

The new batch is mapped in `docs/sales-content-coverage.csv`; the original remains in `docs/business-content-coverage.csv`.

## Reader experience

- Business hub adds sales/customer relationships, sales leadership/operations and strategy classics.
- Sales playbook follows prospecting, qualification, discovery, demonstration, concerns, agreement and customer support.
- The question bank filters by conversation stage and searches prompt/context text. All prompts are present in the initial server-rendered view. Filtering is local and does not store or transmit input.
- Communication hub groups listening, clarity, evidence-based discussion and reflective reference material. NLP is explicitly neuro-linguistic programming, separate from AI natural language processing.
- The NLP reference retains definitions, examples and course-specific terms in readable glossary cards with limitations preceding the detailed claims.
- Historical chapters use continuous reading paragraphs, thematic subsections, previous/next navigation and separately labeled editorial business reflections. The original military text is preserved without adding modern operational extensions.
- Existing catalogue discovery automatically adds all 49 new URLs to combined XML sitemap, sitemap partitions and human-readable content index.
- Existing catalogue-aware consent/privacy advertising policy continues to apply. No advertising or analytics bypass is introduced.

## Visuals

- Sixteen-dot figure: 4×4 arrangement, with four filled dots at row 1 columns 1–2 and row 3 columns 1–2.
- Nine-dot figure: 3×3 open dots.
- Both exercises lack a stated question, constraints and answer; the site identifies that gap and invents no solution.
- Network figure: every source count for 3–14 participants is retained in a table. The interactive complete-graph diagram supports every corresponding size, with one edge per unordered pair and no self-links.
- Pair counts are checked against n(n−1)/2. The page distinguishes possible connections and coordination complexity from demonstrated business value or a proven business network effect.
- Public visuals use HTML/CSS/SVG; no copied source raster artwork, new production dependency or image-generation service is needed.

## Editorial limitations

Unreferenced sales percentages and training claims remain source claims, not current benchmarks. The dead-donkey and bundled-offer stories are critical discussion material, not endorsed methods. Persona and assessment labels are not treated as diagnoses or fixed individual categories.

Therapeutic descriptions in the NLP collection are retained as claims, not treatment instructions or invented protocols. The Sturt et al. (2012) systematic review is identified with its date and scope; it is not described as a review of every term. The source’s juxtaposition of NLP and EMDR does not establish equivalent methods or evidence.

The Art of War file does not identify a translation or edition. A separate Lionel Giles translation is linked for comparison; an exact edition match is not asserted. Typographical/reordered source wording is retained and disclosed.

## Source-specific reading notes

### Prospecting and nurturing: earn a useful conversation

- The “eight touches” claim has no cited sample or study in these notes. It is retained as an unverified source claim, not an outreach quota.
- Event and hospitality examples are possible contact settings, not evidence that an invitation creates buying intent. “Pick a fight” is retained as a rhetorical source label, not advice to antagonize a prospect.

### Sales qualification: compare BANT, MEDDIC and related prompts

- The source’s “Identify Plan” heading is followed by a description of pain and later uses “Identify Pain”. The heading is preserved with that discrepancy identified.
- MEDDIC and MEDDICC are kept distinct: this source’s MEDDICC adds Competition. It does not document MEDDPICC or a paper-process component.
- PACT, TAS and WOLFE are supplied as brief acronym lists with no original references or scoring methods.

### SPIN Selling: discover needs before presenting solutions

- The brief sales notes describe need-payoff as offering solutions, while the dedicated SPIN notes describe the buyer articulating the benefits. The overview follows the latter; both supplied descriptions remain accessible.

- The 17% performance improvement and 87% training-loss figures lack a study citation and context in this collection. They are preserved as source claims, not verified universal results.
- Claims about senior buyers and the power of particular question types depend on context; they are not absolute rules.
- The suggestions about subconscious rapport are the source author’s reflections, not part of a verified SPIN prescription. Quotations without publication references remain attributed only as supplied.

### Sales question bank: prepare for each buyer conversation

- Bracketed phrases are adaptable placeholders from the source. The buyer is not expected to share confidential information merely because it appears in a question.
- Pricing and legal questions help organize a conversation; they are not contract clauses or legal advice.

### Buying groups: distinguish roles from personality labels

- Director/analyser/socialiser/consensus and the “power/party/peace/perfection” labels are source heuristics, not validated assessments.
- “Make the gatekeeper your salesperson” is retained as source wording; the useful application is respectful cooperation, not exploiting someone’s role.

### Sales objections: understand the concern and respond

- The “58% more objections” statistic is unreferenced. The statement that walking away always loses a deal is a source opinion; a poor-fit opportunity can reasonably end.
- “No is a guidepost” and repeated closing attempts are examined critically. They do not override a buyer’s refusal or consent.

### Closing: agree a decision and a concrete next step

- The claims that 80% of sales require five follow-up calls and 44% of representatives stop after one have no cited evidence here.
- Assumed shipment dates and “dilute influence” are retained for critical study, not recommended as substitutes for explicit agreement.

### Customer retention, referrals and service quality

- The five-times acquisition-cost claim, 86% willingness-to-pay figure and 1% expectations figure lack a traceable study and sample in these notes. They are source claims, not current benchmarks.
- The “four servicing contacts a year” suggestion is not a universal cadence. Agree an appropriate schedule with each customer.
- The NPS sentence ends “recommend a customer”, which appears to be a wording error. Its supplied wording is retained; the related feedback guide supplies the wider context.

### CRM and customer experience: connect insights to service

- This is a functional inventory, not a specification for collecting personal information or an endorsement of any product. “Unpaid armies” is source wording for peer participation, not a recommended description of customers.

### Account planning: retain, expand and coordinate

- Hunter, farmer, technician and consultant are role metaphors in the notes, not fixed personality types.

### Sales forecasting: methods named in the collection

- Only method names are supplied. No formula, example dataset, calculation or claimed forecast accuracy has been invented.

### Sales leadership: purpose, accountability and team learning

- IQ/AQ/TQ/EQ, DISC and MBTI appear as an inventory of assessment ideas, without validation evidence or a hiring method. The source’s “Emotional Quotation” wording is preserved as an apparent label error.

### Value selling and misleading offers: examine the examples

- The dead-donkey raffle and Moutai/house/car story have no documented source or outcome. They are preserved as anecdotes, not factual case studies or recommended sales methods.
- The four performance zones are source labels, not an assessment of any individual.

### Listening and rapport: understand before responding

- Eye contact, proximity, touch and gestures vary with preference, culture and accessibility. Ask and adapt; they are not universal indicators of rapport.

### Clear communication: the seven Cs and visual presentation

- “Understand a slide within three seconds” and “one idea per slide” are design heuristics from the notes, not measured universal thresholds.
- “Bossification” is named without an explanation. No missing definition is invented.

### Persuasion: credibility, emotion, reasoning and evidence

### Needs and motivation: use models as questions

### Innovation: discovery, incubation and acceleration

- “Blue sky”, “jump shot” and “layups” are metaphors in this collection.

### Business model canvas: connect value and economics

### Business responsibility: CSR and the triple bottom line

### Business lessons: flexibility, leverage and profit models

- “Gamble small and win big” and “use future’s money” are source slogans, not financing advice or evidence of favorable returns.
- The dot-com lessons are attributed only as a list; the original author and cases are not given.

### Build, buy or partner: methods for pursuing strategy

- The source’s “build monopoly” list is retained as a strategy claim, not evidence that any company can secure a monopoly or should disregard competition constraints.

### Stakeholders: interests, influence and responsibility

- The new source lists stakeholder types without explaining the model’s power, legitimacy and urgency dimensions; those missing explanations are not invented.

### NLP perspectives and modeling: what the notes propose

- The re-imprinting description claims healing effects but supplies no evidence or procedure. It is retained as a source claim, not a treatment recommendation.
- Modeling another person’s perspective does not establish what that person actually thinks or feels.
- Breathing/body matching, the bell-jar distraction label and the positive-intention assumption are source claims. They do not establish consent, agreement or effectiveness.

### NLP language patterns: clarify assumptions and missing detail

- “Moral Operator” appears to refer to modal language such as must and should; the original term is retained and the possible terminology error is disclosed.
- Claims about bypassing resistance or influencing the unconscious are source descriptions, not established results.

### Communication stances: the Satir categories in the notes

- Satir’s communication categories are presented as the source’s model descriptions. They are not diagnoses or a validated personality assessment.

### Preferences and motivation: an annotated sorting glossary

- This glossary mixes NLP terminology with broader personality, motivation and psychological concepts. It is not one unified or validated taxonomy.
- Descriptions of sensory preferences are source claims, not evidence that matching sensory words improves communication or that people have fixed learning types.
- The source’s “Beware of All Labels” caution is retained alongside the definitions.

### Learning and problem solving: terms from the NLP collection

- Several entries are brief generic descriptions without a complete protocol or original reference.
- “D.V.P.” is expanded as deletions, generalizations and distortions in the source, which does not match its initials. No alternative expansion has been invented.

### Personal development and therapeutic claims: a critical reference

- These entries are descriptions from the supplied notes, not instructions for treating trauma, compulsive behavior, eating difficulties or other health conditions. No therapeutic exercises or missing protocols have been generated.
- The 2012 Sturt et al. systematic review found limited evidence for NLP health outcomes and emphasized the small quantity and low quality of the research. This is a dated evidence note, not a claim that every listed concept has been individually evaluated.
- The source mentions EMDR alongside NLP under reprocessing. That juxtaposition does not establish that the methods, evidence or practitioner requirements are equivalent.
- “Binary code of forgetfulness”, “inner hero” and other named patterns may be metaphors or course-specific labels; original manuals and validation are not supplied.
- The “Inner Peace Questionnaire” is named but contains no actual questionnaire items.

### Dot exercises: examine the assumptions before solving

- The source gives a 4×4 arrangement with four filled dots and a 3×3 arrangement of open dots. It does not provide a task, constraints, answers or explanation of the filled dots. No solution is invented.

### Network connections: how pairwise links grow

- The image models a complete undirected graph, with one possible link per distinct pair and no self-links. Actual social networks can be incomplete, directed or weighted.
- The “network effect” heading does not demonstrate a business network effect or establish that revenue, utility or company value grows with the number of possible pairs.
