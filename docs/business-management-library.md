# Business & Management library

Status: original management hub committed and pushed. Its source material remains preserved; the locally implemented sales/communication extension is described in `docs/sales-library-implementation.md`.

## Source coverage

- Four DOCX files retained byte-for-byte inside `content/sources/business/collection.zip` (the refreshed D.zip).
- 2,677 nonempty source paragraphs, including table-cell paragraphs, mapped to published guide sections.
- 57 topic guides plus one hub, organized into eight categories.
- 20 source images reviewed: 19 rebuilt as accessible HTML diagrams/comparisons; one source rendering error explicitly disclosed.
- The TOWS image also contains cropped endings; visible material is retained and missing text disclosed.
- The inventory records document/media SHA-256 hashes, exact original paragraph text, styles and source table coordinates.
- The coverage CSV records every paragraph and image destination. Structural source headings may be consolidated into meaningful page/section headings; exact repeated list entries may share one displayed entry.
- The build script asserts no unmapped paragraphs/images, no missing nonstructural text after formatting/copy editing, unique slugs and valid related-guide links.
- This is coverage of the supplied material, not certification that historical claims, quotations or examples are correct.

## Reader experience

Hub: `/library/knowledge/business-management/`. Library feature and topic filters lead to the hub. Readers can start with a decision journey or browse all eight categories. Topic pages offer an introduction, takeaway, compact overview, section navigation, detailed notes, visual explanations where supported, source limitations and related reading.

Default library browsing shows hub cards rather than all business and AI articles at once. Selecting the topic or searching exposes individual guides. Full detailed text is searchable. All new article URLs are automatically discovered by the existing combined sitemap and human-readable content index.

Original raster images are retained only inside the archive. Public visuals use themed HTML/CSS, semantic lists and scrollable tables with no new image payload or dependency. Advertising eligibility continues through the existing catalogue-aware layout and consent/privacy policy.

## Editorial limitations

Historical company descriptions, transaction figures, country comparisons and attributed quotations are preserved as collection claims and identified as unverified. No current rankings, legal advice or fabricated market estimates are added.

Selected official further reading was checked for BCG, EOS, Blue Ocean and Kotter. This does not validate unrelated source claims.

### What strategy does—and who makes it

- The original growth-strategy row says “makes no change”; that describes stability rather than growth. The wording is retained below as a source discrepancy, not a definition to follow.
- The attributed definitions are quotations in the supplied notes; their original editions and page references were not supplied.

### Strategy perspectives: planned, emergent and adaptive

- The notes describe resource-led strategy as more certain and market-led strategy as riskier. Treat this as a teaching contrast, not a general risk ranking.
- Several outside-in/inside-out comparisons are deliberately polarized; actual organizations can combine both approaches.

### Understanding your competitive landscape

- The four market structures are listed in the source without definitions; this page preserves that list and does not invent missing course explanations.

### Competitive dynamics: moves, timing and responses

- First-, second- and late-mover outcomes are possibilities, not guaranteed profit or risk rankings.

### Cost, differentiation and focused strategies

- The AirAsia, Lexus and IKEA examples are historical teaching illustrations. The notes do not establish their current operations.

### Market leaders, challengers and followers

- Counterfeiting is retained as a named source classification, not recommended business conduct.

### Strategic groups: map the closest competitors

- The automobile map is a historical teaching example, not a current classification of brands or ownership.
- The “Strategic Customers” subsection lists search directions without explaining a customer-analysis method; no missing method has been invented.

### PESTLE: assess the wider business environment

- Market/non-market tags in the notes are a simplified teaching distinction; factors can interact across these categories.
- Examples involving laws, economies or technologies are prompts from the notes, not current jurisdiction-specific guidance.

### Porter’s Five Forces: assess industry pressure

- The source’s positioning responses are possible actions to evaluate, not guarantees that price cuts or acquisitions improve results.

### TAM, SAM and SOM: define the market you can serve

- The source uses short questions rather than a calculation method; no market estimates or numerical assumptions have been invented.

### Core competencies: build strengths that travel

- The source says core competencies do not depreciate, while also warning of core rigidities. Durability is conditional; capabilities can lose relevance.
- Company examples illustrate the notes’ reasoning and are not verified current capability assessments.

### VRIO and VRIN: evaluate resources and advantage

- The source mixes VRIO with VRIN: non-substitutability is retained as a related criterion, not relabeled as the O in VRIO.

### Value chains, networks and outsourcing

- The source says never outsource core activities. Retain this as a strategic caution about losing critical capabilities, rather than an absolute rule for every business.

### Supply-chain response: mobilize to operate

- The source labels this “Value Chain Analysis – Accenture”, but its content concerns supply-chain disruption response. Original publication details were not supplied.

### Ansoff: choose a direction for growth

- The “Samsung Galaxy 7” wording and the Burger King campaign are retained as historical source examples with incomplete or potentially inaccurate naming. They are not current factual case studies.
- The image suggests a fixed sequence for growth choices. Treat sequencing as the source’s recommendation, not a universal requirement.

### Diversification and integration: extending business scope

- Company holdings, transaction dates and prices are unverified historical notes. Preserve them as source claims, not current facts.

- The source image labels relative share with a percent sign while its ticks are ratios such as 2×. The recreated visual uses relative-share ratios.

### GE matrix: weigh attractiveness against business strength

### Pareto: find the few factors with the greatest impact

## Maintenance

Edit `scripts/content-business/plan.json` for the original management batch’s semantic classification, framing, source ranges and related links. Edit `figures.json` for its reviewed visual transcriptions. Run `python3 scripts/content-business/build.py` to regenerate all retained batches together. New material must be read and mapped before adding its source archive and reviewed fingerprint in `batches.json`; do not replace the management archive. The generator rejects unexpected document names and unreviewed embedded charts, files or equations.

Do not use source ranges as an automated classifier for future material. Read new content and revise its mapping. Source offsets are zero-based indices of nonempty paragraphs in document order.
